Pickleball
Rules & Guidelines

1. General Rules

  • The tournament will be conducted according to the latest USA Pickleball rules.
  • All players are expected to demonstrate good sportsmanship and fair play.
  • The Core Committee's decision (based on the Pickleball Subcommittee) will be final in case of any disputes.

2. Match Format

  • Up to the semi-finals: one game of 21 points. Point no. 21 will be the golden point, which decides the winner.
  • Up to the semi-finals: teams will switch sides when either team reaches 11 points.
  • Semi-finals and finals: best of three games of 11 points. The winner of a game is the team that reaches 11 points first. Point no. 11 will be the golden point.
  • Semi-finals and finals: teams will switch sides in the third game when either team reaches 6 points.
  • The team that wins the toss (e.g. Team A) can choose to serve or choose a side. If Team A chooses to serve, the opposing team can choose their preferred side.
  • One time-out is allowed per match for each team.

3. Scoring

  • Rally point scoring will be used.

4. Serving Rules

  • The serve must be made diagonally into the opponent's service court.
  • Only one serve attempt is allowed.
  • At least one foot must remain behind the baseline during the serve.

5. Two-Bounce Rule

  • The receiving team must allow the serve to bounce before returning it.
  • The serving team must also allow the return of serve to bounce before hitting it.
  • After these two bounces, volleys are allowed while players are outside the Kitchen (Non-Volley Zone).

6. Kitchen (Non-Volley Zone) Rules

  • Players may not volley the ball while standing in the Kitchen.
  • A player cannot step on the Kitchen line during a volley.
  • Momentum carrying a player into the Kitchen after a volley is also a fault.

7. Time Regulations

  • Players must report to the court at least 15 minutes before their scheduled match.
  • Any team that fails to report within 10 minutes of the called match time may forfeit the match, and a walkover will be awarded to the opposing team.

8. Equipment

  • Non-marking court shoes are mandatory.
  • USPA-approved pickleball paddles must be used.

9. Conduct

  • Unsportsmanlike behaviour, abusive language or misconduct may result in a warning or disqualification.

10. Injury & Safety

  • Players participate at their own risk.
  • Tournament organisers are not responsible for injuries sustained during play.

11. Ball Hitting the Roof

  • If the ball touches the roof or any overhead structure during play, it shall be considered a fault.
  • The point will be awarded to the team opposing the player/team that caused the ball to hit the roof.

Carroms
Rules & Guidelines

Objective

To be the player who scores the maximum points by pocketing carrom pieces and the queen.

Playing Rules

  1. Place all 19 carrom pieces within the circle in the middle of the board.
  2. Place your striker between the two striking lines (baseline) on your side. You may also place the striker within the circles at either end of your baseline.
  3. The striker may not “cut the moon” – be placed partly within the baseline and partly within the circle.
  4. When striking, the player's hand or arm must not cross the diagonal foul lines at either end of the baseline.
  5. The first player breaks by flicking the striker forward with the index finger towards the centre formation of carrom pieces.
  6. Each player pockets either white or black pieces for the duration of the game.
  7. If a player pockets a piece, they play again. This continues until they fail to pocket a piece; then it is the opponent's turn.
  8. A piece can be struck directly only if it is not touching the player's baseline or behind it.
  9. If a piece is behind or touching the player's baseline, it must be hit by rebounding the striker off a side of the board or another piece, in a forward direction.
  10. The two arrows/circles on the board in front of the player's striking line are treated as the baseline/circle, and pieces there cannot be struck directly.
  11. No side flicks are permitted. All shots must be in the forward direction.
  12. The queen may be pocketed at any point after the first piece has been pocketed, but before the last piece is pocketed.
  13. To cover the queen, the player must pocket another black or white piece immediately after pocketing the queen. If they fail, the queen is returned to the centre of the board.
  14. Pieces returned to the centre can be placed on top of other pieces within the main circle.
  15. Pieces resting on their edge or overlapping another piece are left as they are until moved in the normal course of play.
  16. If the striker comes to rest under another piece, remove it with as little disturbance to the covering piece as possible.
  17. The winner is the player who scores the maximum points.

Fouls

A foul is penalised with negative points (see Scoring). A foul is recorded when:

  • The striker is pocketed.
  • A black or white piece is pocketed together with the striker: the piece is awarded to the player, but a foul is recorded and the turn passes to the opponent.
  • The queen is pocketed together with the striker: the queen goes back to the centre, a foul is recorded and the turn passes to the opponent.
  • The striker or any other piece leaves the board.
  • A player pockets the final piece before the queen has been covered – the pocketed piece is returned to the centre.
  • A player breaks the rules for striking.
  • A player touches any piece in play other than the striker.
  • A player disturbs the board or coins.

The decision of the referee is final.

Scoring

White piece+2
Black piece+1
Queen (counted only when covered)+5
Each foul−1

The aggregate is the total for white pieces, black pieces and the queen, less the points for fouls. The player with the maximum aggregate wins the game.

8 Ball Pool
Rules & Guidelines

Format

  • Pool is played as doubles, with two teams per association.
  • The team that breaks first is decided by a toss.

The Balls & Objective

  • Balls 1–7 are solids (spots) and balls 9–15 are stripes. The white striker ball is the cue ball. The 8-ball is black and is used by each player to finish the game.
  • The player who pots their own set of balls, followed by the 8-ball, before the opponent, wins the game.

The Break & Choosing Groups

  • For a legal break, the player must hit the balls and the cue ball must not go into a pocket.
  • If the 8-ball is potted on the break, the player may ask for a re-rack.
  • If the break is illegal, the opposing team gets to break.
  • The first player to pot an object ball continues with that group (stripes or solids); the opponent takes the other group.
  • After potting an object ball, the player must touch a ball of the same group on their next strike, otherwise the table is open for the opponent.

Turns

A player keeps shooting until they foul or fail to pot an object ball. Then it is the opponent's turn, and play continues like this for the rest of the game.

Fouls

After a foul, the opponent may place the cue ball anywhere on the table (ball-in-hand). Common fouls:

  • Failing to hit your own object balls.
  • Hitting the cue ball off the table.
  • Hitting the cue ball twice.
  • Pushing the cue ball rather than striking it.
  • Taking a shot when it is not your turn.
  • Missing the black ball when playing for the black ball.

Other Playing Rules

  • The black ball may be potted only in a corner pocket. The player must first nominate the pocket. Failing to do so returns the turn to the opponent; potting the 8-ball in any pocket other than the nominated one loses the game.
  • If the cue ball is potted at any point, the opponent gets the cue ball in hand and may place it anywhere on the table.
  • Players must not touch any ball with their body. A player may lean on the table, but at least one foot must stay on the ground.

Not a Foul

  • Hitting your own ball but potting an opponent's ball in the same shot.
  • Potting an opponent's ball after hitting the black ball, when playing the black ball.

Automatic Loss

The opponent wins if a player:

  • Hits the 8-ball off the table.
  • Pots the 8-ball and the cue ball at the same time.
  • Accidentally pots the 8-ball before potting all of their own balls.
  • Pots the 8-ball in the wrong pocket.

Match Format & Time Limit

  • Because of time constraints, each round (league or knockout) is a single match of exactly 20 minutes.
  • The team or player that pockets more balls in the 20 minutes wins.
  • If both have pocketed the same number of balls, 5 extra minutes are given. If it is still level, the first to pocket any ball wins.
  • If the game is tied with the black ball still on the table, time is extended until the black ball is potted.
  • Semi-finals and finals are best of 3 frames.

Conduct & Reporting

  • Only one team representative and the players of the playing teams, the umpire and the organisers are allowed inside the pool room. Spectators can watch from outside.
  • For any situation not covered by these rules, the umpire will consult both players and team representatives and seek agreement. If there is a dispute, the organising committee members present will decide.
  • Players must report at the venue 15 minutes before their scheduled match time, otherwise a walkover will be given.

Cricket
Rules & Guidelines

Toss & Team

  • The toss for the next match takes place during the innings break of the current match. The toss winner must announce their decision immediately and confirm their Playing 9.
  • No changes to the team are allowed once the Playing 9 is confirmed.
  • An Impact Player option is available. It need not be fixed when the Playing 9 is given – any remaining player in the squad can be brought in. The umpires must be informed when the Impact Player comes on.

Overs & Bowling

  • There is no fixed power play.
  • A bowler can bowl a maximum of 2 overs in a match.
  • In a 7-over innings, a minimum of 5 bowlers must be used.
  • The maximum time to complete 6 overs is 24 + 4 = 28 minutes; no extra time will be given.
  • If a bowler is called for an illegal action (no-ball) by the umpire, they cannot bowl again in that match. If it happens again in the next match, the bowler will not be allowed to bowl in the league.

Ball

  • The batting team selects the balls before the match. A new ball is taken only if those balls are defective or lost, as instructed by the umpires.
  • If the ball breaks during play, the batter can decide whether to cancel the delivery.

Ties

  • No super overs in league matches.
  • In knockout matches, a maximum of 2 super overs per game. If still tied, the winner is decided by a toss.

General

  • All new ICC rules will apply.
  • In a dispute, only the team captain may come onto the ground to speak to the umpire.
  • A team must have at least 7 players to take part. Players not in the team may not field as substitutes.
  • If a team is more than 10 minutes late against the fixture time and the opposing team is ready on the ground, the opposing team will be declared the winner.
  • If both teams arrive late, the match will be cancelled or the overs reduced.
  • Jersey, track lower and shoes are mandatory. Shirts, trousers, jeans, shorts etc. are not allowed.
  • Intoxicants must not be used on the ground under any circumstances.
  • The umpire's decision will be final.
  • The Core Committee has full authority to amend the competition.

Throw Ball
Rules & Guidelines

Audience Guidance

The audience may not enter the court. A maximum of 24 people are allowed inside the court while a game is on:

  • Playing teams (6 each)
  • Referees (2)
  • Scorer (1)
  • Organiser (1)
  • Coach / team representative (1 per playing team)
  • Substitutes (max 2 per team, on rotation)
  • Cameraman / commentator (2)

Audience and supporters can watch the game from outside the court.

Rules of Throw Ball

  • Each team has 6 active players. A maximum of 5 substitutions is allowed.
  • Games are played with an official throwball (size 5).
  • Players must catch the ball with both hands and return it with one hand only.
  • The serve must be made within 5 seconds of the whistle.
  • The serve must not touch the net. During a rally, the ball may touch the net.
  • A player may not take more than 1 step while holding the ball during a rally.
  • Double touches are not allowed while receiving the ball.
  • The ball must not touch any part of the body other than the palms when catching or throwing.
  • The serve must be released from above the shoulder line.
  • During a rally, the ball can touch the front box and players can move freely within the court.
  • A ball caught during a rally must be released within 3 seconds.
  • Two players may not catch the ball at the same time – the team loses the point.
  • A player may jump while throwing, and the ball can touch the net (but not the antenna). Players must be in contact with the ground when catching.
  • Front-row players (closest to the net) may not push the ball down into the opposite court – this is a foul.
  • Balls landing on the lines are “in” and valid.
  • Touching the line while catching or throwing is a foul.
  • Players rotate in a “Z” pattern: after a service change (on conceding a point), the player in position 6 moves to position 1, position 1 to position 2, and so on.
  • Each team has one 2-minute time-out per match. The coach asks the referee verbally or by signalling a “T” with both hands.
  • The referee's decision is final. Disrespect towards the referee or opposing players will lead to disqualification.

Match Format & Court

  • Net height is 2.2 metres.
  • Court dimensions are the same as a standard badminton court.
  • All preliminary games are a single set of 21 points. From the semi-finals, the playing teams may choose best of 3 sets or a single set.

Rules & Guidelines

Rummy Rules

  • A maximum of 6 participants from each association.
  • The game is played on a knock-out basis (Gund in Malayalam): the winner is the player with the lowest minus points. The player who declares gets zero, and the others get minus points according to the points they lose by. There is no positive scoring.
  • Each game is 12 shuffles. The direction of play is reversed after six shuffles.
  • Three packs of cards will be used. Thennali is treated as a natural sequence.
  • Two sequences are compulsory, one of which must be natural. Players without two sequences lose 100 points. For players with two sequences, their actual points are recorded as minus points.
  • Scoot and middle scoot are allowed: scoot is −25 points and middle scoot −50 points.
  • A player who reaches 321 points is out of the competition.
  • The declarer must satisfy the other players that the declaration is correct. A false declaration costs the declarer 100 points.
  • After a declaration, other players may open their hands only once it has been confirmed. If a declaration turns out to be false, any player who opened their hand early loses 100 points, regardless of their actual points.
  • Each round has a maximum time limit of one hour. If a table is behind schedule, the points at the end of the hour (after finishing the game in progress) decide the winners. There is no time limit in the final round.
  • In case of any dispute, the tournament director's decision is binding on all players.
  • The game is common for ladies and gentlemen.

Waterpolo
Rules & Guidelines

Format

  • All games, including the preliminary rounds, are knockout.
  • Each team has 4 players including the goalkeeper. There is no limit on substitutions.
  • Preliminary games: two halves of 5 minutes each, with a 2-minute break.
  • From the semi-finals: two halves of 7 minutes each, with a 2-minute break.
  • If a game ends in a draw, each team takes 3 penalty shots. If still tied, the winner is decided by a coin toss.
  • There is no losers' final. Third place is decided on goal difference in the semi-finals; if there is no clear difference, by a coin toss.

Playing Rules

  • A player with the ball can pass it forwards, sideways or backwards.
  • The ball can be handled with only one hand at a time, except by the goalkeeper, who can use both hands. In the ladies' format, the ball can be handled with both hands.
  • Players may not carry the ball while moving – the ball must stay floating on the water surface.
  • Players must not take the ball under water when tackled.
  • Players must not impede an opponent who is not holding the ball.
  • Pushing off an opponent is not permitted.
  • A goal cannot be scored directly from a throw-in.
  • Misconduct or disrespect towards the referee will not be tolerated.

Dress & Safety

  • Ornaments such as neck chains and finger rings are strictly not allowed in the pool.
  • Only synthetic swimming trunks are allowed.
  • All players of a team must wear caps of a different colour from the opposing team.

Decisions

The referee's decision is final. There is no appeal.

Rules & Guidelines

Rules

  • Only one team per association.
  • Each group must have at least 3 members, with no upper limit – boys, girls, elders or all together. Age no bar.
  • Duration: 10 minutes in total, including entry, stage setting, singing and clearing the stage. Exceeding the time limit means a deduction of 3 points from the overall score.
  • Songs can be in any language. Classical and semi-classical compositions, Hindustani music, and film or non-film tunes are accepted.
  • Up to three instruments are allowed (optional), brought by the participants. No instruments will be provided by the organisers.
  • Karaoke can be used and must be handed to the organisers well in advance.
  • Profanity or inappropriate lyrics will lead to disqualification.
  • A minimum of 5 associations must take part, otherwise the event will be cancelled.

Judging

Teams are judged on music composition, swar, tal, pronunciation and mode of presentation. The judges' decision will be final. Other criteria:

  • Vocal skills: pitch, breath control, dynamics, phrasing, enunciation and tone.
  • Presentation skills: eye contact, body and facial expression, attire and microphone technique.
  • Audience response: based on the judges' observation.
  • Overall impression: at the judges' discretion.

Antakshari
Rules & Guidelines

Teams

  • Each association can register a maximum of 1 team, with 2 to 4 members.
  • Enjoy every moment and take part in good spirits!

General Rules

  • There are only two types of rounds: Direct and Passing.
  • Direct round: the question is not passed on and there are no bonus points. A direct round scores 10 points.
  • Passing round: if a team cannot answer, the question passes to the other teams, and the team that answers gets 5 bonus points.
  • Only Malayalam and Hindi film songs from 1960 to 2024 will be accepted.
  • The anchor/judge's decision is final. The anchor may choose the rounds and amend the rules as needed.
  • Not all of the round types below will be used on the day – the anchor will choose a few.
